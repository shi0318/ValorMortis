If you suspect you missed something in a Valor Mortis chapter, start with a focused revisit rather than a campaign restart. Choose the missing category, identify the last place you actually checked and use the current map or travel options available in your save. Journal pages, echoes and optional discoveries do not all answer the same question. This guide gives you a collection-review method, not a guaranteed all-items route or a claim that every missed interaction remains recoverable from every story state.

## Name the gap before choosing a route

First decide what you are looking for. A missing piece of story information, a locked path and an achievement requirement can look like the same unfinished chapter while requiring different work. Read the journal or objective text that prompted the concern. If the game does not identify an exact total, do not invent a percentage and treat it as proof that a particular room contains the final item. An uncertain gap should remain a question until you can name the evidence behind it.

Write a short target such as review the last unread journal entry or recheck the blocked passage I marked earlier. A target narrow enough to finish is easier to investigate than the vague instruction clear the whole region again. If you are chasing a trophy, read its exact requirement separately. This page does not replace the achievement checklist, and it does not assume every collectible is linked to an achievement. Keeping the target specific prevents an ordinary revisit from turning into hours of unrelated searching.

## Make a compact record of visited spaces

Use the location names and travel points actually shown in your current game. For each useful area, record whether you passed through it, searched it deliberately or left a branch unresolved. Those are different states. Walking down a corridor during a fight is not the same as inspecting it after the encounter. A note that distinguishes the two can reveal a worthwhile revisit without pretending you remember every corner of a visually dense chapter.

Avoid drawing connections you have not tested. A doorway seen from one side may not establish an accessible shortcut, and an attractive background space may not be playable. Record the obstacle or visible interaction rather than an imagined destination. When you return, update the note with what the game actually lets you do. This makes your record useful even when you cannot complete the branch yet, because you will know the specific question to ask after acquiring a new ability.

## Return with a reason after gaining an ability

Valor Mortis uses exploration and later abilities to open additional opportunities in previously visited areas. That supports revisiting a remembered obstacle, but it does not mean every new ability opens every blocked route. Before traveling, describe the obstacle you want to test and read the current ability description. If the connection is only a theory, label it that way. Then make one direct comparison at the location instead of wandering the entire chapter expecting every locked space to change.

If the path remains unavailable, preserve the observation and move on to another target. Repeating the same input does not establish that the game is bugged, and a route remaining closed does not prove the item is permanently missable. You may lack a different condition or have misunderstood the environment. A focused note gives a future walkthrough or support question enough detail to help, whereas a general statement that a chapter cannot be completed provides very little to investigate.

## Separate collecting an item from understanding its information

A journal page can add context that is easy to overlook while moving quickly. After finding a story item, review the associated text at a suitable moment and note what it actually adds. Do not assume that remembering a scene means you have collected every related entry, or that a gap in your interpretation proves there is another physical page nearby. Narrative ambiguity and an uncollected item are different problems, even when both leave you feeling that something is missing.

The same care applies to echoes and other discoveries. Use the game's visible feedback to establish that an interaction registered. If you are uncertain, check the relevant record or inspect the location again through normal play. Avoid inventing a completion rule based on the sound of one interaction or a video edit. You want a repeatable way to recognize what your save contains, rather than an increasingly confident memory of what you think you must have collected during an earlier encounter.

## Search the branch, then return to the main route

Choose one unresolved branch and give it a clear endpoint. You might inspect a side passage, check the space beyond an obstacle or read a previously skipped interaction. When that target is resolved, return to a known route before choosing another. This reduces repeated searching of the same room and makes it easier to remember why you entered a side area. A compact route with a specific question is usually easier to audit than an unbroken sweep across several chapters.

Keep combat preparation separate from the collection note. If a route is difficult to reach, use the appropriate combat or chapter guide for that encounter; do not turn a collection checklist into an unverified shortcut through enemies. A successful run should leave you able to say which area you checked and what changed. If you die before reaching it, retain the target and adjust the approach. Losing the attempt does not erase the information that made the revisit worthwhile.

## Do not confuse a patched quest with an automatic completion update

Some official patch guidance specifies that affected side quests require returning to their locations rather than waiting for the journal to resolve by itself. That is a useful reminder when a missing entry is tied to a named quest. Check the relevant current quest guide and the exact fix before assuming that installing an update adds everything you missed. Collection cleanup, quest initiation and a broken progression state should remain separate diagnoses.

If the problem is a boss reward after simultaneous death, a blocked chapter transition or a missing quest interaction, use the targeted recovery instructions instead of repeating a general collection sweep. Record the chapter, objective, recent actions and current patch state. This preserves a useful report if the problem persists. It also avoids treating a genuine technical issue as an invisible collectible, or treating an unexplored side room as evidence that your save is damaged.

## Preserve your save before making irreversible experiments

Use supported backup or platform procedures when you need to protect valuable progress. Do not install an unknown completed save merely to compare collection states. Another player's save may differ in choices, quest progress, upgrades or version, and it can hide rather than explain the missing element in your own run. Keep the campaign you understand intact while you investigate a narrow question. A backup is protection, not proof that every action is reversible inside the game.

Avoid declaring the entire game free of missables on the basis of an early chapter guide. A statement about one part of the campaign does not automatically apply to every later interaction. If a future step could advance the story, finish the checks that matter to you before proceeding or consult a clearly relevant walkthrough. Where reliable information is absent, say that the condition is unknown rather than offering a comforting but unsupported promise that you can always return.

## Frequently asked questions

Does this provide an exact chapter completion counter? No. It is a method for identifying your missing category and checking actual locations; it does not invent a tracker the game has not shown. Should I restart because I cannot find one entry? First inspect your journal, the unresolved branch record and the relevant chapter or quest guidance. A restart should not be the first response to a gap you have not yet defined.

Is this a new November collectible patch? No. Recent player discussion includes a request for better information about what is missing in chapters; that motivates the question, not a claim that a feature has been added. The November 12 official-update check still leads with the October 27 announcement. Use the current interface, name the gap, revisit one plausible place and record the result. That process gives you useful progress without promising a secret counter, a perfect map or a universal recovery rule.
