Start from the objective and route shown in your current game, then decide whether the next useful step is exploration, encounter practice or a return to an earlier junction. Valor Mortis combines first-person combat with interconnected exploration and new mobility. A useful progression plan connects those systems without pretending that a short page can verify every chapter, pickup and boss location.

This guide provides a repeatable method for moving forward. It is not a complete collectible route, a verified eleven-chapter checklist or a promise that every earlier area remains accessible forever. Keep your own objective text and observed routes as the record for the save you are actually playing.

## Establish where your attempt begins

Before leaving a safe point, read the current objective and check the route you used to arrive. Identify the last junction you understand, the encounter still unresolved and any exit you have already opened. Those three details give you a practical starting point after a death or a break from the game.

Use the location names displayed by your build. A name in an older guide may describe a different stage, a translated label or an assumed region. If the name does not match, compare the actual route and objective before following more precise instructions. Never overwrite an important save merely to make your situation resemble an online walkthrough.

Set one immediate goal: reach a visible exit, understand an enemy sequence, inspect a blocked route or confirm whether a previously noticed path is now usable. Trying to clear every side area and win a difficult fight during the same unfamiliar attempt makes it harder to understand where progress stopped.

## Read a junction before taking the risk

At a branching route, distinguish an obvious continuation from an optional-looking passage without assuming that appearances prove the game's intended order. Look for the objective, a recognizable connection and any access requirement. If the path requires a traversal action you have not learned, record it rather than repeatedly attempting an unsupported jump.

A useful route note names the nearest landmark, the direction you approached from and the visible obstacle. Avoid writing only a chapter number. A number will not tell you which doorway you meant when you return several sessions later.

| Observation | Record | Next check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A route seems blocked | The actual obstacle and nearest landmark | Does the game show an ability or interaction requirement? |
| A door appears to lead back | Its relation to the known route | Can you confirm the connection from both sides? |
| An optional encounter is costly | Supplies and actions required to reach it | Is learning it now useful for your present goal? |
| A pickup seems inaccessible | Its position and approach | Has a newly gained movement option changed access? |

The table is an exploration aid. It does not establish that every blocked path can later be opened, that every item is recoverable or that an apparent shortcut is safe before you inspect it.

## Learn an encounter before extending the route

When combat repeatedly interrupts exploration, identify the first failure rather than judging the entire region as too difficult. Were you surprised from the side, committed to a long attack, missed a defensive cue or unable to see the exit? Improve that first problem before increasing the distance of your attempt.

For several enemies, keep the immediate threat and your retreat space in view. The [positioning guide](/multi-enemy-positioning-threat-control/) explains that practice in more detail. For a duel, shorten your first response so that you can observe the end of the sequence before attempting a larger punish.

Do not assume every glowing attack can be blocked or parried. Follow the game's tutorial and the behavior you can verify. A video from a different build or a dramatic animation is not enough to establish a precise input window. The [combat practice guide](/rapier-saber-critical-combat-practice/) separates defensive learning from repeated attack commitment.

## Prepare for a boss attempt as a separate task

Before entering a fight you expect to repeat, inspect your equipped weapon, available recovery and intended opening. Understand how you will regain space if that opening fails. A first attempt can be useful for observing the arena even when you do not expect to win it.

Watch the route behind you as well as the enemy. Repeated backward movement can take you into a boundary or out of useful camera space. On the next attempt, choose a recognizable part of the arena as your reference rather than copying a move sequence without knowing where you are.

When your failure is reproducible, change one relevant decision: the approach, the length of your attack or the moment you disengage. Upgrades may help, but they should support a clearly identified need. The [Catalyst and skills guide](/guides/best-skills-guide/) explains how to choose a purchase without inventing a universal best build.

## Revisit deliberately rather than promising complete recovery

When you gain a movement option, review the unresolved routes you recorded. Compare the new action with the actual obstacle. A higher ledge, a gap and a locked interaction may need different solutions; one upgrade does not prove that all three are now accessible.

Before a major transition, inspect nearby unresolved passages that are reasonably reachable. This is a conservative planning habit, not proof that the next transition is a permanent point of no return. If the game gives a warning, read it before confirming. If it does not, avoid inventing a guarantee about later access.

For collection checks, review your junction notes and the unresolved-route checklist above. Keep a personal list of observed pickups and unfinished routes. An achievement not unlocking does not, by itself, identify the exact missing object or prove the save is damaged.

## Separate a route problem from a technical problem

A frozen image, a missing interaction and being unsure which path to take are different symptoms. For a technical failure, record the build, platform, location and last action before trying a recovery. Preserve the existing save and the error message.

Do not delete configuration or save folders as an exploration experiment. Use the [PC troubleshooting guide](/pc-stutter-after-halloween-update/) for performance symptoms, and the [chapter recovery guide](/boss-achievement-and-chapter-recovery/) for known patch-related boundaries and careful reporting. A historical fix is not a guarantee that every later failure has the same cause.

End a session with a short handoff to yourself: current objective, last known safe route, first unresolved obstacle and the next action you intend to try. That makes the next session more useful than an enormous unverified list of supposed chapter facts, and keeps your decisions tied to the actual state of your game.

## Story and setting

The official premise identifies William as a soldier of Napoleon's Grande Armée, resurrected from a battlefield grave into a plague-corrupted Europe. The game uses a nineteenth-century alternate history and supernatural horror; it is not a historical record of Napoleon's campaigns. The same corruption gives William powers and makes his resurrection part of the conflict he is investigating.

For the story in your playthrough, keep the current objective separate from optional dialogue and collectible writing. A character's statement can be an in-world perspective rather than an objective explanation. Record where you heard an important claim before comparing it with a later revelation. This guide does not publish an independently verified complete cast or assign unsupported faction roles to every name in preview coverage.
