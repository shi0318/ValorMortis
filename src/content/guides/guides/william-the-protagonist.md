You play **William**, a fallen soldier of Napoleon's Grande Armée, in a 19th-century Eastern Europe the store page describes as twisted by dark magic. That is the protagonist. Searches for "main character," "protagonist," and the first-person hands are the same question. The hands hold a blade and a gun at the same time. They are not two characters, and they are not two wallets.

Valor Mortis launched **October 13, 2026**. On **October 20** it was **$39.99** with no discount, Mostly Positive at **1,602** positive out of **2,054**. One More Level, the studio behind Ghostrunner, shipped a first-person soulslike. A review at **1,551** minutes liked the left-hand gun and right-hand melee, and bought it for the Napoleonic setting. A review at **1,771** minutes called it Napoleon souls and talked about parry-focused first-person combat. Both are describing William. Neither is describing a party you switch.

## One budget

Upgrade materials are finite. The achievement list is built around committing. **Bladesmith**, one melee weapon maxed, sat around **8.7%**. **Gun of Glory**, one firearm maxed, sat around **6.5%**. **Transmutation Artifex** was lower still. Those rates are the argument against maxing the rapier and the pistol in the first street. Pick the hand you parry with. Put the first materials there. The other hand waits until the first weapon has a hit you trust.

The rapier is the parry tool. The pistol spends a recovery. Stasis and fire are the pack answers, not the opener. [Parry before the shot](/guides/parry-before-the-shot/). [Stasis is for the pack](/guides/when-to-use-stasis/). William does not get a third body to hold a third build. [Three builds](/guides/three-builds-not-one-tree/) means three ways to spend the same soldier, not three soldiers.

## What the first week changed around him

The October 16 update did not rename the protagonist. It fixed a Chapter 4 save after the boss cutscene, a Chapter 12 lantern edge case, and a Chapter 5 sidequest, In the Name of Art, that could fail to start. It also stopped Volunteer enemies from freezing after a patrol backstab. "No more free Volunteer kills," in the note's words. William still backstabs. The volunteers still fight back after the patch. Parry them. Do not look for a second character to do it.

Demo and playtest players with stutters were told to delete `GameUserSettings.ini`, not the save, and not the character. A deleted settings file does not create a new protagonist. It creates a fresh graphics file. Update the GPU driver if the new popup says the driver is old. The studio said drivers were one of the largest stability gaps in the first days. A hitch is not a lore mystery.

## Names the searches add

`mimi` and `teddy` show up as queries beside the protagonist searches. This page will not invent a relationship, a quest step, or a stat block for either name. If they are in your journal, follow the journal. If they are not, they are not required to understand who you are. William is required. The Grande Armée framing is required. A side name without a sentence in the current build is a later page.

**39** achievements. **RookieMistake** was at **67.4%** on October 20. **AbyssAccepted** was **62.4%**. **TheBookOfBeasts** was **7.4%**. The early pops are the soldier learning the street. The rare ones are not a second protagonist. Open the list after you have a weapon you meant to max. Do not open it looking for a character-select screen the store page never sold.

Minimum spec is **16 GB** of RAM, a GTX 1660 Ti or RX 5600 XT, and **60 GB** on an SSD, about 30 frames at 1080p on Low with upscaling. William's parry does not need the reviewer's 4K frame-generation setup. It needs a frame that shows the swing. Delete the old ini if you came from the demo and the street stutters. Then parry. The **$39.99** soldier is one pair of hands. Spend the materials like it.


## One more pass before you close the menu

The numbers on this page were checked against the October 20, 2026 Steam listing, not against a launch-week memory. Price, review count, achievement total, and the patch lines named above are the facts to plan a session around. A thumbnail that contradicts them is a thumbnail. Play the build you have. Write down the result in the game's own words. Then decide whether the next door, the next bind, or the next purchase is the one this page was about.

If the machine is under the minimum spec printed on the store page, fix that before you fix the tactic. An SSD requirement is a disk requirement. A RAM number is a RAM number. A driver popup is a driver popup. None of those become optional because a guide got longer. The tactic still matters after the machine holds a frame. It does not matter during a hitch you mistook for an enemy tell.

Stop when the one test in the list above has a yes or a no. A second system, a second cosmetic, and a second difficulty change in the same minute will make the yes useless. Come back for the second change after the first one is written down. The rest of the site can wait until that note exists.

William is one soldier with two hands. The rapier and the pistol share the materials. Mimi and Teddy stay in the journal if the journal names them, and out of this page if it does not. The October 16 patch did not add a second protagonist. It saved a Chapter 4 cutscene, fixed a Chapter 5 quest start, and asked demo players to delete one settings file. None of that is a character select. Parry with the blade. Spend the gun on a window you already earned. The Napoleonic pitch on the store page is the pitch. The $39.99 purchase is that soldier, not a party.

Keep the file on the account that will finish it. William's materials do not split across a second pair of hands. One soldier, one budget, one street at a time.
