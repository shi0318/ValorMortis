Buy the vial that addresses your next encounter before restocking grenades. In the November 23 retail patch, Protective Vial and Cleansing Vial dropped to **300 each**, while Grenade rose to **400** and Oil Grenade to **500**. Use the table to set aside the exact amount you need for consumables and keep the remainder for a level or upgrade. The prices below come from the [developer’s November 23 Steam announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2828710/announcements/). Match them against your installed build and current shop before purchasing.

## What changed at the merchant

| Item | Buy before → after | Sell before → after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Protective Vial | 6,000 → 300 | 2,000 → 150 |
| Cleansing Vial | 1,200 → 300 | 400 → 150 |
| Grenade | 200 → 400 | 100 → 200 |
| Oil Grenade | 300 → 500 | 150 → 250 |
| Refreshing Vial | Buy price unlisted in this patch | 100 → 150 |

The numbers are in-game prices. The patch lists a change to the **sale** value of Refreshing Vial. Use the merchant screen for its purchase price and for any items outside the table. The announcement documents price changes separately from combat balance changes and supplies no new damage, duration or application values for these items.

If you have an old shopping list, compare it one line at a time. Someone who avoided Protective Vials at 6,000 has a very different decision at 300. Anyone who used to budget 500 for one regular Grenade and one Oil Grenade needs 900 for the same pair after the update. A plan to sell a spare Protective Vial to fund a large purchase also needs revising, because the old 2,000 sale value is now 150.

## Decide what to buy before the next attempt

1. **Read the loss that keeps repeating.** After a failed attempt, identify what happened immediately before the run got away from you. If you were interrupted while lining up an action, buying a different item may leave the same problem. If the inventory description matches a specific effect you keep encountering, a vial may be worth trying.
2. **Count what you already carry.** Check the inventory, then read the exact item name on the merchant screen. Protective, Cleansing, and Refreshing Vials are separate listings. If you already have a suitable supply for the test, try that before buying more.
3. **Reserve the next upgrade first.** Check the displayed requirement at the point where you intend to spend. See the [Catalysts and skills guide](/guides/best-skills-guide/) for how the upgrade screen works. From the currency you currently hold, subtract that amount or bank it first if the upgrade is available. Consider the leftover amount your supply budget.
4. **Buy for a single comparison.** Try the item in a familiar encounter and note whether it changed the problem you bought it for. If you never found a moment to use it, work on your timing first. If it solved a repeatable problem, that is a reason to stock another for the next stretch.

If you have plenty of healing left when the run ends, identify what caused the loss before filling every consumable slot. A new defensive item has low value when the main problem is losing sight of enemies or ending your attack animation in a crowded corner. Use the [group combat positioning guide](/multi-enemy-positioning-threat-control/) to get a stable baseline, then see whether a consumable improves it.

## Example budget with 1,000 Catalysts to spare

The following budget is an **illustration**, using the published post-patch prices. Assume you already set aside any currency you need for your next upgrade, the relevant items are available, and you have 1,000 left to spend.

- **One vial and one regular Grenade cost 700.** Keep the remaining 300 for another vial after the first test or carry it toward your next upgrade.
- **Two vials and one regular Grenade cost 1,000.** This uses the entire supply budget. It only makes sense when you can explain why you need both vials on the next run.
- **One vial and one Oil Grenade cost 800.** You keep 200. If the oil option has no specific job in your loadout, compare the extra 100 with what you want to save.
- **One of each grenade costs 900.** The remaining 100 cannot buy one of the two discounted vials, so choose this basket when you already have enough of the defensive item you want to carry.

Use the price your shop actually shows if it differs. Keep one named purchase, one intended use, and the remaining balance in mind when comparing baskets. The cheapest option can still waste currency when it is for a situation you rarely encounter.

## When selling makes sense

Check the sell preview for the exact item and quantity. After the patch, selling one Protective or Cleansing Vial returns 150. Replacing it costs 300 if it is available at the listed price. Selling a regular Grenade and re-buying it costs a net 200. The same round trip on an Oil Grenade costs 250. If you plan to use an item soon, keeping it saves you that difference.

If you only need a small amount to reach an immediate upgrade, check whether the item on the sell screen is truly spare. A Refreshing Vial now brings in 150, but you need its local buy price to know what replenishing it would cost. Read the trade preview again after switching between the buy and sell panes before accepting. An old value in a community table can be a useful clue that the table predates the patch.

## If your shop looks different

Check that you are using the retail release and that your platform finished installing the current update. Reopen the shop and compare the buy and sell lines for a named item. A missing listing is a separate symptom from a changed price. The patch price table establishes values for the named items when offered. It gives no universal stock list or story unlock route.

If prices still differ, record the platform, installed build, seller, item name and buy or sell mode. Those details let another player compare the same listing.
